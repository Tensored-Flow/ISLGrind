Let $n$ be a positive integer, and let $A$ be a subset of $\{1, \ldots, n\}$. An \emph{$A$-partition} of $n$ into $k$ \emph{parts} is a representation of $n$ as a sum $n = a_1 + \cdots + a_k$, where the parts $a_1, \ldots, a_k$ belong to $A$ and are not necessarily distinct. The \emph{number of different parts} in such a partition is the number of (distinct) elements in the set $\{a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_k\}$.

We say that an $A$-partition of $n$ into $k$ parts is \emph{optimal} if there is no $A$-partition of $n$ into $r$ parts with $r < k$. Prove that any optimal $A$-partition of $n$ contains at most $\sqrt[3]{6n}$ different parts.
