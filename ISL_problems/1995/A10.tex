For positive integers $n$, the numbers $f(n)$ are defined inductively as follows: $f(1) = 1$, and for every positive integer $n$, $f(n+1)$ is the greatest integer $m$ such that there is an arithmetic progression of positive integers $a_1 < a_2 < \cdots < a_m = n$ for which
\[ f(a_1) = f(a_2) = \cdots = f(a_m). \]
Prove that there are positive integers $a$ and $b$ such that $f(an+b) = n+2$ for every positive integer $n$.
