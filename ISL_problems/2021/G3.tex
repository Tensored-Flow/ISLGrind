\textit{Version 1.} Let $n$ be a fixed positive integer, and let $\mathsf{S}$ be the set of points $(x,y)$ on the Cartesian plane such that both coordinates $x$ and $y$ are nonnegative integers smaller than $2n$ (thus $|\mathsf{S}| = 4n^2$). Assume that $\mathcal{F}$ is a set consisting of $n^2$ quadrilaterals such that all their vertices lie in $\mathsf{S}$, and each point in $\mathsf{S}$ is a vertex of exactly one of the quadrilaterals in $\mathcal{F}$.

Determine the largest possible sum of areas of all $n^2$ quadrilaterals in $\mathcal{F}$.

\textit{Version 2.} Let $n$ be a fixed positive integer, and let $\mathsf{S}$ be the set of points $(x,y)$ on the Cartesian plane such that both coordinates $x$ and $y$ are nonnegative integers smaller than $2n$ (thus $|\mathsf{S}| = 4n^2$). Assume that $\mathcal{F}$ is a set of polygons such that all vertices of polygons in $\mathcal{F}$ lie in $\mathsf{S}$, and each point in $\mathsf{S}$ is a vertex of exactly one of the polygons in $\mathcal{F}$.

Determine the largest possible total sum of areas of all polygons in $\mathcal{F}$.
