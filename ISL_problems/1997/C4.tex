An $n \times n$ matrix with entries from $\{1, 2, \ldots, 2n-1\}$ is called a \textit{coveralls matrix} if for each $i$ the union of the $i$th row and the $i$th column contains $2n-1$ distinct entries. Show that:
(a) There exist no coveralls matrices for $n = 1997$.
(b) Coveralls matrices exist for infinitely many values of $n$.
