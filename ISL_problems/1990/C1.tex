Given $n$ countries with three representatives each, $m$ committees $A(1), A(2), \ldots, A(m)$ are called a \emph{cycle} if
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] each committee has $n$ members, one from each country;
\item[(ii)] no two committees have the same membership;
\item[(iii)] for $i = 1, 2, \ldots, m$, committee $A(i)$ and committee $A(i+1)$ have no member in common, where $A(m+1)$ denotes $A(1)$;
\item[(iv)] if $1 < |i - j| < m - 1$, then committees $A(i)$ and $A(j)$ have at least one member in common.
\end{enumerate}
Is it possible to have a cycle of 1990 committees with 11 countries?
