Let $U = \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$, where $n \geq 3$. A subset $S$ of $U$ is said to be \emph{split} by an arrangement of the elements of $U$ if an element not in $S$ occurs in the arrangement somewhere between two elements of $S$. For example, $13542$ splits $\{1, 3, 5\}$ but not $\{3, 4, 5\}$. Prove that for any $n - 2$ subsets of $U$, each containing at least 2 and at most $n - 1$ elements, there is an arrangement of the elements of $U$ that splits all of them.
