Let $\alpha(n)$ be the number of digits equal to one in the binary representation of a positive number $n$. Prove that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] the inequality $\alpha(n^2) \leq \frac{1}{2}\alpha(n)(\alpha(n)+1)$ holds;
\item[(b)] the above inequality is an equality for infinitely many positive integers;
\item[(c)] there exists a sequence $(n_i)_1^{\infty}$ such that $\alpha(n_i^2)/\alpha(n_i) \to 0$ as $i \to \infty$.
\end{enumerate}
Alternative parts: Prove that there exists a sequence $(n_i)_1^{\infty}$ such that $\alpha(n_i^2)/\alpha(n_i)$ tends to
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(d)] $\infty$;
\item[(e)] an arbitrary real number $\gamma \in (0,1)$;
\item[(f)] an arbitrary real number $\gamma \geq 0$.
\end{enumerate}
